# Opening the company's Google Play developer account

**Who this is for:** the person in the organisation who will register the account — usually someone in
administration, finance or legal, with access to the company's registration documents. No technical knowledge
is needed.

**What we are asking:** to open a Google Play *developer account in the company's name*, so that our mobile app,
**Cosarathi Capture**, can be published on the Google Play Store. The app team cannot do this on the company's
behalf: Google verifies the legal organisation and the person registering it.

## Before anything else: do we already have one?

Ask IT, or whoever manages the company's websites and apps, two questions:

1. *Does the company already have a Google Play Console developer account?*
2. *Does the company already have a D-U-N-S number?*

If the answer to the first is yes, **stop here**: ask the account's owner to invite the app team (step 6) and
skip everything else. If only the second is yes, skip step 1.

---

## Step 1 — Get the company's D-U-N-S number *(the long wait — start today)*

A D-U-N-S number is a free nine-digit identifier for a business, issued by Dun & Bradstreet. Google uses it to
confirm that the organisation is real. Apple uses the same number, so we will need it again for the iPhone app —
please keep it on file.

1. **Check whether one already exists.** Many registered companies have one without knowing. Search for the
   company on Dun & Bradstreet's website for your country (in India, `dnb.co.in`), or ask the finance team —
   it sometimes appears on credit or supplier paperwork.
2. **If there is none, request one.** It is free through Dun & Bradstreet's standard request. You will be asked
   for the legal company name, registered address, phone number, the name of a director or owner, the type of
   business and the number of employees.
3. **Wait.** Allow anywhere from a few working days to about three weeks. Dun & Bradstreet offers a paid
   fast-track if we are in a hurry — please ask the app team before paying for it.

> **The detail that matters most:** the legal name and registered address in the D-U-N-S record must match the
> company's registration documents *exactly* — spelling, punctuation, "Pvt Ltd" versus "Private Limited". Google
> copies them from that record, and a mismatch is the most common reason an application gets stuck. If the
> existing record is out of date, ask Dun & Bradstreet to correct it **before** moving to step 4.

## Step 2 — Create a company Google account to own it

The developer account will belong to whichever Google account registers it, so it must not be anyone's personal
Gmail.

- Create or choose a **company-controlled address**, for example `mobile@ourcompany.com` — ideally a shared
  mailbox that more than one person can read.
- Turn on **2-Step Verification** for it. Google requires this.
- Make sure the recovery phone number and recovery email also belong to the company, not to one employee.

## Step 3 — Have these ready

- [ ] The D-U-N-S number
- [ ] Legal company name and registered address, exactly as in the D-U-N-S record
- [ ] The company's **website** address — it must be live
- [ ] An **email address on the company's domain** and a **phone number** that Google can show to the public
- [ ] A contact person's name, email and phone — Google sends a verification code to both
- [ ] A company document: certificate of incorporation, GST registration, or similar
- [ ] Government photo ID of the person registering
- [ ] A company card for the US$25 fee

## Step 4 — Register

1. Signed in with the company Google account from step 2, go to **`play.google.com/console/signup`**.
2. When asked what kind of account, choose **"An organisation"** — not "Yourself". This matters: personal
   accounts have to run a two-week test programme before they may publish anything, and organisation accounts
   do not.
3. Enter the **D-U-N-S number**. Google fills in the company name and address from it. Check them.
4. Choose the **developer name**. This is the name the public sees on the Play Store under the app. Please use:
   **[developer name to be confirmed by the app team]**
5. Fill in the organisation's details and the contact details. Verify the email and phone with the codes Google
   sends.
6. Accept the developer agreement and pay the US$25 registration fee.

## Step 5 — Verification

Google will then ask you to prove two things: that the **person** registering is real, and that the
**organisation** is real. Expect to upload the government ID and the company document from step 3. Google may
also ask you to confirm that the company owns its website — if so, send that request to IT or to the app team;
it takes a few minutes for whoever manages the site.

Verification usually takes a few days. Google emails the account's address with the result or with questions,
so **please watch that mailbox** — an unanswered question is the second most common reason for delay.

## Step 6 — Give the app team access

Once the account is verified:

1. In Play Console, open **Users and permissions** → **Invite new users**.
2. Invite: **[app team email address(es)]**
3. Give them **Admin** permission for now. It can be narrowed to just this app once the app exists.

The account stays the company's. The app team only gets permission to work inside it, and that permission can
be withdrawn at any time.

## What will be publicly visible

Anyone looking at the app on the Play Store will be able to see the developer name, the company's legal name and
address, a contact email, and the website. Assume anything Google labels "public" is public, and use company
contact details rather than a personal phone number or address.

## What to send back to the app team

- [ ] The D-U-N-S number — we need it again later for Apple
- [ ] Confirmation that the account is verified
- [ ] The exact developer name that was registered
- [ ] Confirmation that the invitation in step 6 has been sent
- [ ] Who inside the company owns the account from now on, in case that person changes role

## If it gets stuck

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Google rejects the company name or address | It differs from the D-U-N-S record. Have Dun & Bradstreet correct the record, then try again. |
| "D-U-N-S number not found" | A new number can take a few days to reach Google. Wait two or three days and retry. |
| The document is rejected | Upload a clearer scan, and check it shows the same legal name and address as the D-U-N-S record. |
| No word from Google for over a week | Look in the account's mailbox, including spam, for a question; then use the help option inside Play Console. |
| A screen looks different from this guide | Google changes its wording from time to time. Follow what the screen says, and tell the app team what differed. |

## Later: the iPhone App Store

Not now — there is an annual fee, so we will ask when we are ready. For planning purposes: Apple's Developer
Program for organisations costs **US$99 a year**, uses the **same D-U-N-S number**, requires the person enrolling
to have the legal authority to sign agreements for the company (or to name someone who does), and takes about one
to two weeks to verify. Keeping the D-U-N-S number and these documents on file will make it quick.
